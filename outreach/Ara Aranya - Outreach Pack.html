How to approach a brand

Getting the first yes

Every message below is written and ready. Swap in the highlighted parts and send. The templates are short on purpose — a brand manager reads the first two lines and decides. Everything after that only matters if those two lines worked.

Six rules that decide whether you get a reply

  1. Lead with them, not you. "I've been using your cleanser for three weeks" beats "I am a UGC creator with a passion for beauty." One proves you know them; the other could have been sent to anyone.
  2. Never open with your rate. Price answers a question they have not asked yet. Get them interested first, quote second.
  3. One ask per message. Not "let me know if you want UGC, live hosting, photography or a collab." Pick the one thing that fits them and ask for that.
  4. Say where you are. Kota Bharu is an advantage, not an apology. Local brands want someone who can actually turn up; remote brands want someone whose rates are not KL rates.
  5. Show, do not describe. One link to your work beats a paragraph about your style.
  6. Follow up once, then stop. One nudge after five working days. If nothing, move on — and try again in three months when their campaign cycle turns over.

Send 10 a week, not 50 in one night. Ten researched messages beat fifty copy-pastes, and a burst of identical DMs is how accounts get flagged as spam. Ten a week is forty a month — at a normal reply rate that is a handful of real conversations, which is all you need.

When they reply

Talking about money

These are the replies that decide what you actually get paid. Having the words ready is the difference between holding your rate and agreeing to something at midnight you regret in the morning.

"What are your rates?"

Attach the rate card with thisHi name, thanks for coming back to me. My full rate card is attached. For what you've described, the closest fit is package or line item — name it, at RM figure. That covers concept, script, filming, editing and captions, with organic usage included. Paid advertising usage is licensed separately, so tell me if you need it and I'll fold it into the quote. Happy to put a firm number in writing once I know the deliverables and the deadline. Ara

Always name one line from the card. Sending nine pages and letting them guess is how a quote turns into a negotiation. Point at the number you want.

"We only do barter / gifting"

Copy thisThanks for coming back to me. I do take gifted collaborations occasionally, but only where I approached the brand — because then the product was my choice and the risk is mine. For briefs that come from a brand, the content is paid work: you choose the product, set the price and hold the stock, and none of those are things I control. My single video starts at RM 400, and gifted product can come off that. If the budget genuinely isn't there this quarter, tell me when it is and I'll keep the door open. Ara

When they reply

Holding your ground

"Can you do it cheaper?"

Copy thisI can work to most budgets — but by changing the scope, not the rate. For their budget I could do a smaller deliverable — one video instead of three, 30 seconds instead of 60, organic usage only instead of paid. Which matters more to you: the number of videos, or the usage rights? Tell me and I'll shape it around that.

Never just lower the number. The moment you discount without removing something, your rate card becomes a suggestion — and that brand will open at the discount every time after. Cut scope, hold price.

"Send us your portfolio"

Copy thisOf course — everything is here: novabadzlinaglobal.com The Work page has my video and photography, and there's a voice demo page if audio is relevant. I've attached my rate card too so you have the numbers in front of you. If you tell me the product and roughly when you'd need it, I'll come back with a firm quote rather than a range.

They said yes, then went quiet

Copy thisHi name — just checking in on project. I have date held for you at the moment. If the timeline has moved, no problem at all — just let me know so I can open the slot up. Ara

That last one does real work. It is polite, it is not needy, and it quietly tells them the date is worth something. Most silences are internal delays rather than rejections — but the slot is still yours to lose.
